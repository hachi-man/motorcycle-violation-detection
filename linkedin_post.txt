Today I completed my first end-to-end Computer Vision project as part of the AIRI Team PITB AI Internship Task 1.

I built a Motorcycle Traffic Violation Detection System using YOLOv8 — a system that automatically detects helmet non-compliance and motorcycle overloading (triple riding) from road footage.

What makes this meaningful: both violations are on Punjab's Safe City e-challan enforcement roadmap. I built a working prototype of something PITB is actively trying to deploy.

The project covered the complete AI workflow:
→ Problem definition aligned with real government enforcement needs
→ Dataset collection from Roboflow, Kaggle, and Pakistani road footage
→ Manual annotation of 578 images across 4 classes using Roboflow
→ 6 training experiments comparing YOLOv8n vs YOLOv8s across different configurations
→ Model evaluation with Precision, Recall, mAP@0.5, and mAP@0.5:0.95
→ Inference on unseen test images with custom OpenCV visualization
→ Error analysis identifying 13 failure cases with root causes and improvements

Best model: YOLOv8n, 40 epochs, batch=16 → mAP@0.5: 0.926

Key finding from experiments: YOLOv8s (11M parameters) performed worse than YOLOv8n (3M parameters) on this dataset — confirming that model complexity must match dataset size. Bigger is not always better.

The biggest lesson: a good AI model depends not just on the algorithm, but on clean data, consistent annotation, honest evaluation, and understanding why the model fails.

Tools used: Python, Google Colab, YOLOv8, Ultralytics, Roboflow, OpenCV, PyTorch, Matplotlib
